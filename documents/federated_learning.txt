Federated learning is a way to train a machine learning model across many devices (like phones or hospitals' local servers) without ever moving the raw data to a central server. Instead of sending your data to a company's servers to train a model, the model itself is sent to your device, trained locally on your data, and only the updated model parameters (not your raw data) are sent back and combined with everyone else's updates.

The main motivation is privacy: sensitive data (medical records, personal phone data, financial records) never leaves the device it lives on. A central server aggregates the model updates from thousands of devices into one improved global model, then sends that improved model back out for another round of local training. This cycle repeats many times.

Federated learning introduces its own challenges: devices have different amounts and types of data (non-uniform data), some devices drop out mid-training (unreliable connectivity), and there's a risk that model updates themselves can leak information about the underlying data if not handled carefully (which is why federated learning is often combined with techniques like differential privacy). It's used in production today for things like predictive keyboard text on phones, where user typing data is sensitive and shouldn't leave the device.
